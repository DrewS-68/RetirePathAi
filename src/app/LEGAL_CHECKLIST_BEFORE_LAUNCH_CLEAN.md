PART 1: CRITICAL - MUST HAVE LEGAL REVIEW

These areas absolutely require professional legal advice before launch:

PRIORITY 1: Disclaimers & Liability Protection

Why Critical:
- You're providing contract analysis without being a law firm
- You're providing home valuations without being licensed valuers
- You're serving seniors (vulnerable consumers - higher duty of care)
- Risk of liability claims if users rely on incorrect information

What Needs Review:

1. Disclaimer Language:
   - "Not legal advice" disclaimers
   - "Not financial advice" disclaimers
   - AI-generated content disclaimers
   - Limitations of liability
   - User responsibility clauses

2. Placement of Disclaimers:
   - Where disclaimers appear (every page? pop-ups? tool entrances?)
   - How prominent they need to be
   - Whether users must acknowledge before using tools
   - Frequency of reminders

3. Effectiveness of Disclaimers:
   - Are your disclaimers legally enforceable in Australia?
   - Do they actually protect you from liability?
   - Are they clear enough for seniors to understand?

Current Status in Your App:
- Disclaimers in Footer (Terms of Service)
- No disclaimers at point of use (Contract Review tool)
- No disclaimers on AI-generated content
- No acknowledgment checkboxes before using tools
- No disclaimers on Home Valuation estimates

Lawyer Type: Consumer law specialist or tech/startup lawyer
Estimated Cost: $2,000 - $4,000


PRIORITY 2: Terms of Service & Privacy Policy

Why Critical:
- Legally required under Australian Consumer Law
- Privacy Act 1988 compliance mandatory
- GDPR compliance (if any European users)
- Protects you in disputes
- Required by payment processors (Stripe)
- Required by hosting providers (Supabase)

What Needs Review:

1. Terms of Service:
   - Is it comprehensive for your business model?
   - Does it adequately limit liability?
   - Are payment terms clear and compliant?
   - Subscription terms (non-renewal) clearly stated?
   - Intellectual property protections
   - User conduct rules
   - Termination clauses
   - Dispute resolution process
   - Governing law and jurisdiction

2. Privacy Policy:
   - Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) compliance
   - Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) compliance
   - What data you collect
   - How you use, store, and protect data
   - Third-party data sharing (Stripe, Supabase, Resend)
   - User rights (access, correction, deletion)
   - Data breach procedures
   - Cookies and tracking
   - Cross-border data transfers
   - Children's privacy (though not applicable here)

3. Cookie Policy:
   - What cookies you use
   - Consent requirements
   - Opt-out mechanisms

Current Status in Your App:
- Basic Terms of Service in Footer (needs professional review)
- Basic Privacy Policy in Footer (needs professional review)
- Not customized for your specific business model
- Not reviewed by Australian lawyer
- No cookie consent banner
- No data breach response plan

Lawyer Type: Privacy lawyer or tech/startup lawyer with privacy expertise
Estimated Cost: $1,500 - $3,000


PRIORITY 3: Australian Consumer Law Compliance

Why Critical:
- You're dealing with seniors (vulnerable consumers)
- ACCC has strict rules about misleading/deceptive conduct
- Unfair contract terms legislation
- Consumer guarantees apply to your services
- Heavy penalties for non-compliance

What Needs Review:

1. Misleading or Deceptive Conduct (Section 18, ACL):
   - Are your marketing claims accurate?
   - Are your AI "accuracy" claims defensible?
   - Home valuation estimates - are disclaimers sufficient?
   - "Risk scores" for contracts - could these mislead?
   - Village "ratings" - are they objective?

2. Unconscionable Conduct:
   - Are your terms fair for elderly users?
   - Is pricing transparent and reasonable?
   - Are cancellation/refund terms fair?
   - Do you take advantage of vulnerability?

3. Unfair Contract Terms:
   - Do your T&Cs have unfair terms?
   - Unilateral variation clauses
   - Unilateral termination rights
   - Excessive liability limitations
   - Renewal terms (you're okay here - non-auto-renewing)

4. Consumer Guarantees:
   - Services must be fit for purpose
   - Must be delivered with due care and skill
   - Must be completed in reasonable time
   - Your liability cannot be excluded for these

5. Vulnerable Consumers:
   - ACCC specifically protects seniors
   - Higher duty of care required
   - Must ensure services aren't exploitative
   - Clear, simple language required

Current Status in Your App:
- Marketing claims need review ("Smart" Village Matcher)
- AI accuracy claims need substantiation
- Home valuation disclaimers may be insufficient
- Non-auto-renewing subscriptions (good for fairness)
- Refund policy not clearly stated

Lawyer Type: Consumer law specialist familiar with ACCC regulations
Estimated Cost: $2,000 - $4,000


PRIORITY 4: Unauthorized Legal/Financial Advice

Why Critical:
- Providing legal advice without a license is illegal
- Providing financial advice requires AFSL (Australian Financial Services License)
- Property valuations may require licensing
- You could face prosecution and fines

What Needs Review:

1. Legal Advice Boundaries:
   - Does your "Contract Review" tool cross into legal advice?
   - Are your disclaimers sufficient to avoid this classification?
   - Do you need to restrict certain features?
   - Should you require users to consult lawyers?

2. Financial Advice Boundaries:
   - Does home valuation constitute financial advice?
   - Do fee calculations constitute financial advice?
   - Do you need an AFSL or exemption?
   - Are disclaimers sufficient?

3. Property Valuation Licensing:
   - Do you need a property valuer's license?
   - Is AI estimation exempt?
   - What disclaimers are required?
   - Can you use terms like "valuation" or should you say "estimate"?

4. Recommended Changes:
   - Change "Contract Review" to "Contract Analyzer" or "Contract Checker"
   - Change "Home Valuation" to "Home Value Estimator"
   - Add stronger "not advice" disclaimers
   - Require acknowledgment before tool use
   - Add prominent "Consult a professional" messaging

Current Status in Your App:
- "Contract Review" name could imply legal review
- "Home Valuation" could imply licensed valuation
- No mandatory acknowledgment before using tools
- Risk scoring could be seen as advice
- Village recommendations could be seen as advice

Lawyer Type: Regulatory lawyer with ASIC/licensing expertise
Estimated Cost: $2,000 - $5,000

PART 10: BUSINESS REGISTRATION STEPS

If Not Already Done:

1. Choose Business Structure:
- Sole Trader: Simple but no liability protection (NOT RECOMMENDED)
- Company (Pty Ltd): Liability protection, recommended (BEST OPTION)
- Trust: Complex, usually for asset protection

2. Register Company (if choosing Pty Ltd):
- Go to: asic.gov.au
- Register online: $538 fee (as of 2024)
- Choose company name: "RetirePath Pty Ltd"
- Appoint directors
- Issue shares
- Receive ACN (Australian Company Number)

3. Register Business Name:
- If using "RetirePath" as trading name
- Register via: business.gov.au
- Cost: $37/year or $87/3 years

4. Get ABN:
- Free at: abr.gov.au
- Immediate online
- Needed for invoicing and tax

5. Register for GST (if required):
- Required if turnover over $75,000/year
- Register via: ato.gov.au
- You'll need to charge GST on services

6. Trademark (Optional but Recommended):
- Protects "RetirePath" name
- Register via: ipaustralia.gov.au
- Cost: $330-400 per class
- Takes 6-9 months
- Recommended: Engage IP lawyer

PART 12: RED FLAGS TO AVOID

Common Mistakes Startups Make:

MISTAKE: "We'll fix legal stuff after launch"
- Fixing later is 10x more expensive
- Harder to implement with existing users
- Compliance issues can kill traction

MISTAKE: "I copied T&Cs from another site"
- Every business is different
- May not fit your model
- Copyright infringement
- Won't hold up in court

MISTAKE: "Our disclaimers protect us completely"
- Disclaimers have limits
- Can't exclude consumer guarantees
- May not be enforceable if unclear
- Don't replace proper compliance

MISTAKE: "We're too small to be sued"
- Small businesses get sued often
- One claim could bankrupt you
- Seniors are more likely to seek legal recourse
- Village operators might sue over reviews

MISTAKE: "We'll get insurance later"
- Can't get retroactive coverage
- Expensive to add later
- Some claims happen early

MISTAKE: "AI disclaimers are enough"
- AI liability is new legal territory
- Extra caution needed
- Standard disclaimers may not cover AI risks
