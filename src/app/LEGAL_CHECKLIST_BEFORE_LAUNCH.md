**Why Critical:**

- You're providing home valuations without being licensed valuers

**What Needs Review:**

   - Whether users must acknowledge before using tools
   - Frequency of reminders

3. **Effectiveness of Disclaimers:**

   - Do they actually protect you from liability?
   - Are they clear enough for seniors to understand?

- ❌ No disclaimers on AI-generated content

### 🔴 PRIORITY 3: Australian Consumer Law Compliance

**Why Critical:**
- You're dealing with seniors (vulnerable consumers)
- ACCC has strict rules about misleading/deceptive conduct
- Unfair contract terms legislation
- Consumer guarantees apply to your services
- Heavy penalties for non-compliance

**What Needs Review:**

1. **Misleading or Deceptive Conduct (Section 18, ACL):**
   - Are your marketing claims accurate?
   - Are your AI "accuracy" claims defensible?
   - Home valuation estimates - are disclaimers sufficient?
   - "Risk scores" for contracts - could these mislead?
   - Village "ratings" - are they objective?

2. **Unconscionable Conduct:**
   - Are your terms fair for elderly users?
   - Is pricing transparent and reasonable?
   - Are cancellation/refund terms fair?
   - Do you take advantage of vulnerability?

3. **Unfair Contract Terms:**
   - Do your T&Cs have unfair terms?
   - Unilateral variation clauses
   - Unilateral termination rights
   - Excessive liability limitations
   - Renewal terms (you're okay here - non-auto-renewing)

4. **Consumer Guarantees:**
   - Services must be fit for purpose
   - Must be delivered with due care and skill
   - Must be completed in reasonable time
   - Your liability cannot be excluded for these

5. **Vulnerable Consumers:**
   - ACCC specifically protects seniors
   - Higher duty of care required
   - Must ensure services aren't exploitative
   - Clear, simple language required

**Current Status in Your App:**
- ⚠️ Marketing claims need review ("Smart" Village Matcher)
- ⚠️ AI accuracy claims need substantiation
- ⚠️ Home valuation disclaimers may be insufficient
- ✅ Non-auto-renewing subscriptions (good for fairness)
- ⚠️ Refund policy not clearly stated

**Lawyer Type:** Consumer law specialist familiar with ACCC regulations

**Estimated Cost:** $2,000 - $4,000

### 🔴 PRIORITY 4: Unauthorized Legal/Financial Advice

**Why Critical:**
- Providing legal advice without a license is illegal
- Providing financial advice requires AFSL (Australian Financial Services License)
- Property valuations may require licensing
- You could face prosecution and fines

**What Needs Review:**

1. **Legal Advice Boundaries:**
   - Does your "Contract Review" tool cross into legal advice?
   - Are your disclaimers sufficient to avoid this classification?
   - Do you need to restrict certain features?
   - Should you require users to consult lawyers?

2. **Financial Advice Boundaries:**
   - Does home valuation constitute financial advice?
   - Do fee calculations constitute financial advice?
   - Do you need an AFSL or exemption?
   - Are disclaimers sufficient?

3. **Property Valuation Licensing:**
   - Do you need a property valuer's license?
   - Is AI estimation exempt?
   - What disclaimers are required?
   - Can you use terms like "valuation" or should you say "estimate"?

4. **Recommended Changes:**
   - Change "Contract Review" to "Contract Analyzer" or "Contract Checker"
   - Change "Home Valuation" to "Home Value Estimator"
   - Add stronger "not advice" disclaimers
   - Require acknowledgment before tool use
   - Add prominent "Consult a professional" messaging

**Current Status in Your App:**
- ⚠️ "Contract Review" name could imply legal review
- ⚠️ "Home Valuation" could imply licensed valuation
- ❌ No mandatory acknowledgment before using tools
- ⚠️ Risk scoring could be seen as advice
- ⚠️ Village recommendations could be seen as advice

**Lawyer Type:** Regulatory lawyer with ASIC/licensing expertise

**Estimated Cost:** $2,000 - $5,000

## PART 12: RED FLAGS TO AVOID

### Common Mistakes Startups Make:

❌ **"We'll fix legal stuff after launch"**
- Fixing later is 10x more expensive
- Harder to implement with existing users
- Compliance issues can kill traction

❌ **"I copied T&Cs from another site"**
- Every business is different
- May not fit your model
- Copyright infringement
- Won't hold up in court

❌ **"Our disclaimers protect us completely"**
- Disclaimers have limits
- Can't exclude consumer guarantees
- May not be enforceable if unclear
- Don't replace proper compliance

❌ **"We're too small to be sued"**
- Small businesses get sued often
- One claim could bankrupt you
- Seniors are more likely to seek legal recourse
- Village operators might sue over reviews

❌ **"We'll get insurance later"**
- Can't get retroactive coverage
- Expensive to add later
- Some claims happen early

❌ **"AI disclaimers are enough"**
- AI liability is new legal territory
- Extra caution needed
- Standard disclaimers may not cover AI risks

## PART 16: CRISIS SCENARIOS & LEGAL RESPONSE

### What If...

**Scenario 1: User sues claiming your contract analysis was wrong**

Immediate steps:
1. Contact your lawyer immediately
2. Contact your insurer immediately
3. Do NOT admit fault
4. Do NOT contact the user directly
5. Preserve all records and communications
6. Let lawyer and insurer handle

**Scenario 2: Village operator threatens to sue over negative review**

Immediate steps:
1. Review the review for defamation
2. If clearly defamatory, consider removing
3. If opinion/fair comment, may be protected
4. Consult lawyer before responding
5. Have take-down procedure ready
6. Document everything

**Scenario 3: ACCC investigates for misleading conduct**

Immediate steps:
1. Engage lawyer immediately (critical)
2. Cooperate fully with investigation
3. Provide all requested information
4. Do NOT destroy any records
5. Review all marketing materials
6. Prepare to modify claims if needed

**Scenario 4: Data breach - user data exposed**

Immediate steps:
1. Contain the breach immediately
2. Notify affected users within 72 hours (if serious)
3. Notify OAIC if required (serious data breach)
4. Contact cyber insurance provider
5. Engage security expert to investigate
6. Consult lawyer on notification requirements
7. Document everything

**Scenario 5: User complains about "unauthorized advice"**

Immediate steps:
1. Review their specific complaint
2. Check what information was provided
3. Point to disclaimers
4. Offer refund to resolve
5. If escalates, consult lawyer
6. Review and strengthen disclaimers
